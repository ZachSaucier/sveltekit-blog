I have held a diverse set of full-time roles within the tech world. These roles have ranged from being a jack-of-all-trades at a 2-person product company to being a lead developer for some of the best marketing sites in the world.

While I think this diverse experience is a benefit for basically every role I could apply to, it's sometimes hard to talk about effectively, especially in the span of a 30-minute interview when the interview questions aren't made for people like me.

As such, I thought I should think through explicitly what I think makes me stand out from other engineers. After noodling a bit, I came up with this list:

## Differences that I'd argue are good
 - ***I am skilled at noticing.***<br>I usually approach topics with a grander prespective than most engineers. Instead of focusing on only the details in front of me I tend to analyze the whole situation. I am skilled at noticing things that many engineers miss and usually act on these areas of opportunity. An opportunity might be one for a product, a team, or individuals.<span class="excerpt_marker"></span>
 - ***I don't let myself be bound by my role.***<br>Often times the areas of opportunity that I notice are outside of my job description but I act on them for the good of the company and team when appropriate. I love working closely with product managers, designers, and project managers and often act as these roles when needed.
 - ***I believe that details matter.***<br>One small improvement to a function, PR, process, or a kind word said to a teammate may not matter in of itself. But if I'm able to continuously make small improvements (while still getting the big goals accomplished) they compound to things that are truly great, even if the resulting "why" it's great is hard to put into words. Consequently I don't believe in fully-autonomous AI agents for most tasks.
 - ***I want lots of the responsibility.***<br>I want responsibility for my area, both the good and bad. I believe in small teams, high ownership, and high trust. I *love* leading small teams.
 - ***I crave time free from all other responsibilities.***<br>I think it's better for everyone when I have time to think about areas outside of my directives. In these times I come up with my most valuable ideas. I try to build margin into my workload so that I can do this. 
 - ***I need high autonomy.***<br>In order to make my best ideas into reality, I need the flexibility to work on things that I think are most important.
 - ***I care.***<br>I care deeply about things that matter and minimize my time spent on other things. I do my very best to improve my area, first and foremost for customers and my team. I always pay attention to feedback (often doing [my own product support](/blog/customer-support-is-not-thriving/)). I feel compelled to work hard and get things done.
 - ***I have a diverse work history.***<br>I've worked at a 2-person company, an 18,000 person company, and companies in-between. I've helped startups find product-market fit, helped established products redefine themselves, and made top-of-class, cutting-edge web experiences.
 - ***I am well read.***<br>I regularly read not only about software engineering topics but also read about graphics programming, video game history and techniques, art, managing people well, politics, and religion. I continuously add to repositories of inspirational material for visual projects even though my day job hasn't been mostly focused on visuals in years.
 - ***I communicate well.***<br>I am one of the most efficient people you'll meet. I minimize meetings, create exceptional documentation, and am clear and direct when communicating with others. I help those around me do the same.
 - ***I am skilled at getting the best out of others.***<br>I have extensive mentorship experience both inside and outside of my occupation. I pride myself on how often I'm able to set up my teammates for doing their best work by giving them opportunities to grow and helping them accomplish their goals.

In summary, my best attribute is helping teams run significantly better. When I am part of a team, I help the whole team produce higher-quality work more quickly. The more leadership I am given, the more I am able to help.

Secondarily, my mix of product, design, project management, and engineering expertise affords me the ability to work across a diverse set of roles and be helpful to most any team.

## Areas of weakness

- ***Sometimes I'm too harsh.***<br>I have high expectations and don't always communicate well, especially when I am disappointed (though I have dramatically improved in this area).
- ***Sometimes I spend too much time on nitpicks.***<br>Focusing on the most important things and focusing on useless things is a spectrum, not binary. While I think I usually focus on the right things, sometimes my sense of what is important is blurred by my personal preferences. 
- ***Sometimes I'm too focused.***<br>At times I am too focused on what I'm doing and don't think about what's around me enough. Sometimes that means I fail to listen to someone well. Other times that means I don't pivot soon enough.
- ***I'm a specialist, not a unicorn.***<br>With experience spanning product, design, front-end, full-stack, and team leadership, it’s easy for people to expect that I can do everything. But for any one of these areas I can point to a domain purist who is better at it than me. My value isn't in any one of these areas. It's the rare ability to operate at a very high level across all of them.
